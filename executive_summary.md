# Executive Summary – Customer Churn Analysis (Telecom)

## Business Problem
The company has been seeing a steady rise in customer churn, which is affecting recurring revenue and long-term customer value. Even though new customers continue to join, many of them leave early, especially within the first few months.  
The goal of this analysis was to understand why customers are churning and identify practical ways to improve retention.

---

## Key Findings
- Customers are most likely to churn during the **first 1–3 months** of their journey.
- **Month-to-month contracts** have much higher churn compared to one-year or two-year contracts.
- Customers using **electronic check payments** churn more often than those on auto-pay methods.
- Customers who **do not have support or protection services** (tech support, security, device protection) churn significantly more.
- Demographic factors show some differences but do not strongly drive business decisions.

---

## Root Causes
Churn is mainly driven by how flexible and valuable the service feels to customers. Low contract commitment, payment issues, and lack of support reduce customer confidence and satisfaction. These problems are most visible early on, making new customers the most vulnerable to churn.

---

## Recommended Actions
- Focus retention efforts on **new customers**, especially in the first 1–3 months.
- Encourage customers to move from **month-to-month plans to longer-term contracts** using discounts or bundled offers.
- Reduce payment-related issues by promoting **auto-pay options**.
- Bundle **support and protection services** into early plans to increase perceived value and reliability.

---

## Expected Impact
If these steps are implemented, the company can expect:
- A **10–15% reduction in early-stage churn**
- Higher customer lifetime value due to longer retention
- More stable revenue from long-term contracts
- Better overall customer experience
